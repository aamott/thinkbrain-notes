Month

One dot per matching entry, three at most, then +N. The dots say something happened and nothing more — no value gets a colour, because the vocabulary is the user's and the app has no opinion about which mood outranks which (D4).

Today is marked. The selected day is filled and outlined, and stays selected while you page through months, because it is a filter rather than a cursor.

Clicking a day

A day is a filter, not a link. Clicking one filters the popout to that day and never opens an entry — with eight entries on Aug 7 there is no single note to open, and picking one would be a guess.

If the popout is closed, it opens (D59). The day arrives as a chip you can dismiss on its own, and dismissing it clears the calendar's selection in the same movement (D60).

Keyboard, and the phone

The grid is one tab stop with roving focus, so tabbing past a month never means 31 stops. The focused day is drawn with a dashed outline; it announces its date and its exact count.

The one question — what does the tab do when you reopen it?

D56 makes the tab a singleton and persists the view mode and active date per workspace. So reopening the calendar next week lands you on the month you were last looking at, which may be months behind today.

Recommended: persist the view mode, but open on today's month

The view mode is a preference; the date you were last parked on is usually an accident of browsing. Today is one click away either way, but landing in March when it is August is a small daily annoyance. This narrows D56 rather than contradicting it — say the word if you would rather keep the date too.
