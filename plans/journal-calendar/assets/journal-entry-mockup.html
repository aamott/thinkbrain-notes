Why today

Where a new field has to start today

In Settings, or by typing frontmatter by hand. Both work, and neither is where you are when the thought arrives. You are on the entry, you want to note the weather, and the only route is a different screen.

Meanwhile the metadata is on screen twice — once as the dateline, once as the raw block the dateline is a rendering of.

Adding a field where you are

One row at the bottom of the expanded dateline. Open it and you get the fields you have already set up but have not used on this entry; type something else and you get that, as a new key on this note.

Why free text, and not a kind chooser here

Asking "is this one from a list, several from a list, a number, or a few words?" on the page you came to write on is the settings form wearing a disguise. Free text records anything and mangles nothing; when the field earns a place in your settings, that is where its shape gets chosen. A number typed into a free-text field is still a number when you promote it.

Not reading the same thing twice

Live preview already hides # and ** and shows them back when the cursor arrives. Frontmatter should keep the same bargain instead of being the one piece of syntax that stays raw.

Three things to decide

1 · Where does ＋ Add a field live?

The mockup puts it at the bottom of the expanded dateline, so it appears only once you have opened the metadata. The alternative is always visible on the collapsed dateline, one click closer but permanently on a page meant to be mostly prose.

Recommendation — inside the expanded editor, as drawn

2 · Does a field made from a note ask for its kind?

Drawn as free text, with the shape chosen later if you promote it in Settings. Asking here means putting the settings form on the writing page.

Recommendation — free text, promote to choose a shape

3 · Hide frontmatter in every note, or only journal entries?

Drawn as every note with live preview on. Hiding it only where a journal dateline is showing would make the editor's appearance depend on which extension happens to be active — hard to explain, harder to predict.

The cost is that a non-journal note with frontmatter you care about needs a click to see it. Live preview already makes that trade for every other piece of syntax.

Recommendation — every note, revealed on cursor
